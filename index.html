Analyzing Baldur's Gate 3

A game analysis project analyzing design and narrative elements of Baldur's Gate 3. Created by Jade Short.

Element #1 — Game Selection & Brief Rationale

Baldur's Gate 3 is a computer role-playing game developed by Larian Studios. It is based in the Dungeons & Dragons universe. The game follows the protagonist on a journey through the Forgotten Realms to rid themselves (and their party members) of a tadpole infecting their brain. It takes place in a world reminiscent of the old days before our modern tech, but with the addition of magic and unique species of beings. The game explores themes of corruption, power, companionship, morality, and more. Why Baldur's Gate 3? This game, to me, is the perfect example of a game allowing players tons of agency while maintaining a strong storyline. It felt more than fitting to analyze a game that bridges narrative design and player freedom so well. I also just really love this game.

Element #2 — Game Design & Core Elements

Game Design

Baldur's Gate 3 is a role-playing game designed to function similar to the popular table-top game, Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition. It includes choice-based dialogue and turn-based combat. It takes place in a high-fantasy world where magic runs wild and various species of beings co-exist (or don't). The main goal of the story is to rid yourself of a tadpole infection, but the game encourages the player to develop other goals as well, like developing a relationship with certain NPCs, leveling up your character and strengthening their build, recruiting companions (and optionally, learn more about them and aid them in their own storylines) and more. The game provides in depth character customization - you can choose from a variety of races, subraces, classes (your character's style of fighting/playing), subclasses, and backgrounds (slight lore for your character that provides skill points based on which one you choose). The game involves lots of narrative text (Domsch, 2013), more than most games I have played. This occurs both in the form of a narrator and in distant NPC conversation. The narrative text in this game helps to provide lore, immerse the player in the gameworld, and direct the player on certain paths. The game involves many passive elements, that Domsch would describe as non-interactable elements that build narrative or lore, such as cut-scenes, loading screens accompanied with lore, fantasy background music, and more (Domsch, 2013).

Element #3 — Gameplay & Interaction

Interactive Elements and Game Mechanics

Players take turns playing each of the characters in their party. On a turn, the player can make one action and one bonus action (some classes provide an extra action), and have limited movement. Players make actions corresponding with the character they are playing - these can be spells, weapon attacks, item uses, and more. One crucial element of the game is dice-rolling. Dice-rolling occurs exploration, dialogue, and in combat. Players roll a d20 (20-sided die), combined with whatever skill-point additions (or subtractions) they have to determine whether or not they are successful with an action - this could be deceiving an NPC, unlocking a discovered chest, or landing a hit on an enemy. This adds an element of chance to the game similar to Dungeons & Dragons, keeping players on their toes. Baldur's Gate 3 is highly interactive. But, you may ask, does this interactivity affect the game in any meaningful way? Yes. Salen and Zimmeran say that in order to make interactivity in a game meaningful, "experience has to incorporate not just explicit interactivity, but meaningful choice. When a player makes a choice in a game, the system responds in some way. The relationship between the player's choice and the system's response is one way to characterize the depth and quality of interaction," (Salen & Zimmerman, 2004). The feedback given from the choices you make is very strong. Saying certain things or completing certain actions can cause NPCs to react positively or negatively, affecting your character's relationship with them. Your choices genuinely have an impact on the game - you can choose to ignore certain characters or events and you therefore will not experience it in that playthrough. This can prevent the player from encountering certain game events or quests later in the game. As Salen and Zimmerman explain, the game uses many micro-level choices that result in macro-level changes in the game (Salen & Zimmerman, 2024). According to Narain, there are nine types of player choices (Narain, 2016). "The hollow choice" is very present in this game. The character customization options are very in-depth. You can also change your outfit, and even change the color of the clothes you are wearing. "The informed choice" is also very prevalent in the game - like players being given the ability to customize the build of their character's and party's combat styles and the choices presented in combat. In dialogue and exploration, the game presents many "dramatic choices", "weighted choices", and "long-term choices" (Narain, 2016).

Agency & Choices

The map of the game is HUGE. There is endless space to explore, find items, encounter NPCs, etc. Bycer defines emergent gameplay as being present when "the mechanics are flexible enough that they can be combined by the player to produce unintended actions that the developer may or may not have specifically designed the game around," (Bycer, 2015). I think Baldur's Gate 3 is a great example of this. Along with a great amount of freedom to explore, the game also allows mods, allowing players to change the game however they may choose. There are customization mods, gameplay mods, UI mods, combat mechanics mods, and more. One has the option to create almost an entirely new game using mods. To put it simply, Baldur's Gate 3 provides lots of agency to players. The game looks different to everyone, character creation is super in-depth, and the choices you make do make a difference.

Element #4 — Story & Narrative Design

Narrative Structure

As mentioned, the main idea of the game is to rid yourself and your party of a tadpole infestation in your brain. However, as the story progresses, players get the chance to explore the stories of their companions. For example, players can explore Laezel's blind-sighted loyalty, Shadowheart's struggle with her faith, Wyll's difficulty of self-acceptance, and more. Players follow the characters on a journey exploring themes of corruption, dishonesty, loyalty, self-discovery, family, and more. The game is largely based on morals, with dialogue choices and activity choices that allow players to be "good" or "evil" or sometimes neutral, all affecting how other players view you and what shows up later in the game. I feel that there isn't one major story, but rather multiple stories put into one. Interactivity plays a huge part in the structure of the story, as no one playthrough looks the same as another. Like mentioned, the game is highly replayable. You could play the entire game with a completely different party and with a whole new set of encounters thanks to the interactive elements that the game designers built into the game. For example, during your quest to find a cure for the parasite, you are given the mission to make it to Moonrise Towers. You can get there by travelling through the Mountain Pass, or travelling through the Underdark. If you choose to travel through the Underdark, you miss out on lore-building sidequests for one of the NPCs in your party, Laezel (unless you skipped her, rejected her, or killed her). If you choose the Mountain Pass, you miss out on lore related to Shadowheart, another NPC. Your experience of the overall narrative and the individual stories you encounter all depend on the choices you make. The passive elements in the game allow players to discover various different pieces of story. You can find hand-written letters, talk to almost every NPC (of which there are many), find belongings with a backstory, find lost animals (and invite them to your camp!), and more. The gameworld is very open, giving players tons of agency in what the story looks like for them, but it still ties incredibly well back to the main plotline. Because of this, I would describe this game as being a sandbox narrative (Picucci, 2014). Picucci explains that sandbox narratives provide players with a vast open-ended world free from a pre-determined/linear story path and filled with elements to interact with," (Picucci, 2014). In Schell's discussion of branched storyline, he provided an image that I think is a great representation of how the narrative of Baldur's Gate 3 is designed (provided below). Another narrative element that I would like to mention is that the game provides little backstory to your character. You are left, if you choose, to imagine your own narrative for your character and your own reasoning for the choices you make. Schell says, "A good game is a machine that generates stories when people play it," (Schell, 2014). The game not only generates stories through the multiple narrative elements it provides, but also in allowing players to generate their own story.

Element #5 — Player Experience

My Experience

Playing this game makes me feel heavily immersed in the world. I feel like I have tons of power in what I experience while playing. The game mechanics are easy to understand and the main goals of the narrative are clear - and I believe they support each other well. I feel like I have tons of control in what happens in the story, and with every playthrough I end up creating my own stories for my character and the reasoning for their actions. I really love this game. It is one of my favorite games of all time! Because of the complexity of the game and the time it takes to play, I don't always feel like I have the time or energy to sit down and play. But that's less of a critique on the game, and more of a critique on the fast-paced, demanding world we live in. I love how much control I have in the customization of my character - it's one of my favorite aspects of games. I love how I have the ability to form relationships in the game. I love the fantasy theme of the game, and I love the complex narrative the game has. I recommend this game to everyone!
